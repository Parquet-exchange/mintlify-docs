When you close a winning position, Parquet pays you from the pool's free liquidity. Most of the time that's instant and you never think about it. When the pool is briefly short, your unpaid amount becomes a deterministic place in line — not a haircut. This page explains how that works and what you can do with a pending claim.

## The core mechanic

Every winning close is paid from:

```
free_liquidity = total_usdc − reserved_usdc − queue_total_owed
```

If `free_liquidity` covers your payout, you're paid in full and walk away. If it falls short, the unpaid portion is recorded as a `PayoutQueueEntry` PDA, indexed FIFO by `idx`. You pay roughly $0.30 of SOL rent to open the entry. That rent isn't burned — it refunds to whoever later calls `harvest` and drains your entry. See [Glossary: payout queue](/reference/glossary#payout-queue).

<Note>
The queue is per-pool, and every market has its own isolated pool — free liquidity is a property of that market's USDC vault. A queue entry on AAPL drains only as the AAPL pool refills (losing AAPL closes and new AAPL LP deposits); flow on TSLA or MSTR does not touch it.
</Note>

## How the queue pays out

The pool replenishes itself from losing trades. When a losing position closes, the LPs' gain doesn't immediately re-enter the LP balance — it parks in a `side_bucket`. As long as `side_bucket > 0`, anyone can permissionlessly call:

```
harvest(max_entries)
```

This drains queue entries head-first, pays out the claimants, closes the PDA, and refunds rent to the caller. Once the queue is fully drained, `side_bucket` unlocks back into the LP balance.

`max_entries` is a caller-chosen **per-call batch ceiling** (a `u8`, so the wire range is 0–255) that bounds how many non-voided payouts one `harvest` call attempts before stopping — a compute-budget and transaction-size guard, not a queue limit. Voided entries are walked past for free and don't count against it. The keeper passes a small batch (default 8) because larger batches blow the 1232-byte transaction-size cap; you can call `harvest` repeatedly to drain a long queue.

| Actor | Action | Earns |
|-------|--------|-------|
| Winning close (short pool) | Enqueues a `PayoutQueueEntry` | Pays ~$0.30 SOL rent |
| Losing close | Sends LP gain to `side_bucket` | — |
| Anyone | Calls `harvest(max_entries)` | Per-entry PDA rent refund |

Keepers run this loop automatically 24/7, but the call is open — you can run it yourself. The official keeper's pre-flight mirrors the on-chain early-break: it harvests once the side bucket can cover the next entry, so the chain doesn't no-op and the keeper doesn't burn lamports per stuck entry.

<Note>
`harvest` reads the oracle to settle the trader's payout, but under the pyth-hybrid oracle the read succeeds 24/7 — Pyth covers it during RTH, and the relayer-written `PriceFeed` PDA covers it the rest of the time (carrying the off-hours reference). Queue entries drain as soon as `side_bucket` accumulates enough from losing closes, regardless of session.
</Note>

## Your unpaid claim isn't stuck

You don't have to wait for harvest. A `Pending` queue entry can be spent in two ways while it's still in line.

### Open a new position drawing from the queue

In v4, `open_position` accepts a split:

```
wallet_collateral + from_queue_amount
```

The instruction debits part of your new collateral from a pending entry. The draw order is **phantom-first** — it pulls from any `phantom_unpaid_owed` you hold before touching regular `unpaid_owed`. This lets you immediately redeploy a pending claim into a new market without idle time. See [Glossary: phantom credit](/reference/glossary#phantom-credit).

### Drain phantom credit back to your wallet

If you'd rather exit, `drain_phantom_credit` sweeps your phantom balance to your USDC account.

<Warning>
`drain_phantom_credit` only drains once the queue is fully empty. This is intentional: phantom credit is a courtesy ahead of the regular FIFO line, and the protocol won't let it jump live claimants.
</Warning>

## Check your status

The indexer exposes a per-wallet view:

```
GET /queue/user/:wallet
```

Response includes every entry you hold and an aggregate per-market claims summary. Full schema in the [Network API reference](/network/api). If you're building against the queue directly, the [Developer SDK](/developer/sdk) decodes `PayoutQueueEntry` PDAs and exposes harvest helpers.

For the LP-side mechanics — how the queue affects yield, the `side_bucket`, and queue-aware deposits/withdrawals — see [LP payout queue](/developer/lp-payout-queue).

## Queue first, then a bounded backstop

Some earlier perpetual designs socialize losses by clawing back gains from winning positions whenever the pool runs short. Parquet replaces that broad approach with a deterministic FIFO queue: a winning trader either gets paid in full or gets a specific place in line, and the queue drains as losing closes and new LP deposits refill the pool. The queue — not socialization — is the primary mechanism.

Behind the queue sits a **last-resort ADL (auto-deleveraging) tail-backstop**, armed off-hours on all markets. It is *not* the old "claw back every winner" model: it only engages when the unbacked tail of the queue grows past a high trigger and stays aged past a dwell window, and even then it socializes only the **oldest unbacked queue IOUs**, pro-rata, up to a hard per-event and absolute haircut ceiling. It exists so the queue tail cannot grow without bound in an extreme, sustained off-hours imbalance at $0 LP. In normal operation it never fires. See [Risks](/reference/risks#adl-tail-backstop) for the trader-facing disclosure.
